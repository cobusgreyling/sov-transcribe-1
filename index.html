The open-weight transcript you keep.

SOV-Transcribe-1 is a speech model you download. It writes down a recording in seven languages, follows a plain-language instruction, and can be biased toward the names and terms you care about. The weights are Apache 2.0. The audio can stay on your machines.

This is an independent field guide by Cobus Greyling. It is not a Salesforce product, and nothing on this page is a live transcript from the model. Every figure is dated and sourced.

The card sizes the model at 4.6 billion active parameters. The launch post rounds that to 4 billion and calls it six times smaller than Voxtral Small’s 24 billion active. Against the card’s 4.6 billion, twenty-four billion is about five times, not six. Size a machine from the card.

What the weights actually are

A Whisper encoder hears the file. A small projector turns that hearing into tokens. A Gemma 4 mixture-of-experts writes the transcript. Thinking stays off. Decoding is greedy.

1 · Hear

Whisper large-v3

16 kHz mono audio. The reference implementation cuts the file into 30-second windows.

→
2 · Pack

Projector

Four encoder frames are stacked, then a linear layer, GELU, and another linear layer. 375 audio tokens per 30-second window.

→
3 · Write

Gemma 4 26B A4B

The decoder is the instruction-tuned Gemma 4 mixture-of-experts. The router stays in FP32. The base checkpoint is google/gemma-4-26B-A4B-it.

A 30-minute file is 60 windows, which is 22,500 audio tokens before the instruction. The published context is 32,768 tokens. A long transcript can run out of room. Salesforce says to split at speech boundaries when you need to.

Seven languages

English, French, German, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese, Dutch. The launch post calls the English result the headline, and the other six the multilingual set.

EnglishFrenchGermanSpanishItalianPortugueseDutch

One job

Non-streaming automatic speech recognition. You hand it a recording, up to 30 minutes in Salesforce’s tests, plus an instruction. You get text back.

The card does not describe live captions, speaker labels, or translation as a separate task. A voice agent that needs those still has to add them.

Where the 2.28% comes from

Artificial Analysis builds the AA-WER index from about eight hours of English audio. Half the weight is voice-agent talk. A quarter is parliamentary speech. A quarter is earnings calls. Lower is better. The arithmetic below uses Salesforce’s own table, and it lands on 2.28 exactly.

50% AA-AgentTalk · 1.78 25% VoxPopuli-AA · 1.50 25% Earnings22-AA · 4.06

0.50 × 1.78 + 0.25 × 1.50 + 0.25 × 4.06 = 2.28

On agent talk, SOV-Transcribe-1 ties Nemotron 3.5 Transcribe at 1.78. The lead is the earnings calls (4.06 against Nemotron’s 5.69 and Voxtral Small’s 4.78) and the parliamentary set (1.50 against 1.99 and 1.75).

Salesforce’s table, dated with the 12 November model card, ranks it #1 among open weights and #7 overall. Artificial Analysis’s own post that day said #7 of 44. The same organisation’s page, retrieved 13 November 2026, still calls SOV-Transcribe-1 the most accurate open-weights model, at a rounded 2.3%, among 16 open-weights models of 65 evaluated. The rendered summary on that visit did not include a SOV row, so this guide does not invent a fresh overall place.

Hosted systems ahead of a 2.3% index on that 13 November page include Fun-Realtime-ASR-preview at 1.7%, ElevenLabs Scribe v3 at 1.9%, Microsoft MAI-Transcribe-2 at 2.0%, and Zoom Scribe V2.1 Pro and Voxtral Small Transcribe 3 at 2.1%. Grok Voice Transcribe 2.0 is listed at 2.3%. Voxtral Small Transcribe 3 is a different model from the Voxtral Small 24B column in Salesforce’s table.

Four jobs, and the rows that test them

76.33%

The entity has to be perfect

VoiceCodeBench passes a recording only when every target entity is right: a phone number, an email, a URL, a symbol. SOV passes 76.33% of recordings and recovers 93.39% of entities. Nemotron, the only other model filled in, sits at 62.67% and 89.34%. The gap between 93% of entities and 76% of recordings is the product problem. One wrong digit fails the task.

8.21%

Fourteen English accents

On AppTek call-center dialogues the mean word error is 8.21%, just ahead of Nemotron at 8.44% and Qwen at 8.42%, and well ahead of Granite and Voxtral. The launch post says this is the lowest error on 8 of the 14 accents: Australian, British, Scottish, Welsh, Irish, Indian, Mexican, and Singaporean English.

96.8

Names you hand it

On Contextual Earnings-22, keyword F1 for the names in the clip moves from 71.9 with no list to 96.8 with the local list, and holds 94.0 when the list is the whole hour of names, most of them distractors. Word error on the same family of tests moves from 12.42 to 9.73. Qwen’s word error with a local list is lower still, at 9.19.

5.05%

Style you can ask for

Preference-ASR scores whether the transcript follows a requested format. With an instruction, SOV reaches 5.05% preference-aware word error. Granite Speech 5.0 2B is ahead at 4.70%. Without an instruction, SOV leads that table at 5.72%.

Where it separates

  • AA-WER index: 2.28 against 2.77 and 2.81 for the next open weights in the launch table.
  • HALAS, the hallucination-prone earnings audio: 25.69, ahead of Qwen at 26.01 and Nemotron at 31.59. The margin over Qwen is small.
  • Indian English on Monsoon: 2.93, the best cell in that row.
  • Open ASR VoxPopuli subset: 1.46.

Where the table is unkind

  • Multilingual mean: 4.88. Voxtral Small is at 3.39 and Nemotron at 3.87. Dutch is the heavy cell, at 7.67.
  • ContextASR with a fine entity list: 3.67, against Qwen at 1.53 and Nemotron at 1.71. The hint helps SOV, from 7.03 down to 3.67, and other models were already ahead.
  • Clean financial speech on SPGISpeech: 2.41, behind Voxtral at 1.84 and Nemotron at 1.91.
  • AMI meetings: 8.15, behind Granite at 6.96 and Nemotron at 7.78.

What you run

Native vLLM and Transformers support was still being upstreamed on 12 November 2026. Until that lands, Salesforce ships a plugin that installs vLLM 0.32.0, and the repository carries its own Transformers code. trust_remote_code executes that code. Pin the revision you have read.

hf download salesforce-sovereign-intelligence/SOV-Transcribe-1 \
  --include "vllm_plugin/*" --local-dir SOV-Transcribe-1
pip install ./SOV-Transcribe-1/vllm_plugin

vllm serve salesforce-sovereign-intelligence/SOV-Transcribe-1 \
  --max-model-len 32768

curl http://localhost:8000/v1/audio/transcriptions \
  -F model=salesforce-sovereign-intelligence/SOV-Transcribe-1 \
  -F file=@meeting.wav \
  -F temperature=0

The server accepts 600 seconds of audio unless you set VLLM_MAX_AUDIO_DECODE_DURATION_S=1800. Temperature 0 is the recommended decode. The verbatim prompt is the default. Pass -F prompt=... for any other line.

Weights are BF16, with the router in FP32. Holding 26.6 billion BF16 parameters is about 53 GB before the KV cache and the runtime. That figure is arithmetic, not a Salesforce measurement. The published serving test used one NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell with 96 GB.

DFlash, one request at a time

A separate draft, salesforce-sovereign-intelligence/SOV-Transcribe-1-DFlash, proposes 15 tokens for the main model to check. Salesforce reports about a 2–3× decode speedup, smaller on clips under 10 seconds, with accuracy inside run-to-run variation. On a 96 GB GPU they set max_num_seqs to 64 so the draft still fits. Three experiments: the table is one-request latency, 1,039 seconds of audio per second is batch throughput on that GPU, and 60× is the hosted endpoint.

On that same 96 GB GPU, Salesforce reports the draft returning a 30-second file in 0.22 seconds at that throughput. Artificial Analysis measured the hosted endpoint at about one minute of audio in one second.

Keep the decode boring

  • Greedy: temperature 0, top-p 1, top-k 0, repetition penalty 1. In Transformers, do_sample stays false.
  • Thinking stays off: enable_thinking false.
  • Rare loops repeat a character or a phrase until the token limit. Salesforce strips them with a cleanup adapted from the Qwen3-ASR toolkit.
  • Allow up to 8,192 new tokens, and split a long file at speech rather than trusting one 30-minute window.
